Echo Documentation

Echo studies communication-efficient cooperative driving: an autonomous ego vehicle learns when and whom to ask for hidden driver intent, balancing communication costs against driving safety and efficiency.

How Echo Works

  1. Observe Surroundings: The ego vehicle tracks up to 8 spatial neighbor slots within a 60-meter radius on a 4-lane highway.
  2. Select Communication: The Request DQN decides whether to query a neighbor for their hidden intent (target lane, acceleration) or ask nobody to save cost.
  3. Simulate Futures: The MLPx World Model simulates 3-step action trajectories (5³ = 125 candidate paths) conditioned on received messages.
  4. Execute Safely: The Model-Predictive Planner selects the highest-reward action, filtered by an executive safety guard (speed cap and lane-clearance checks).

Key Takeaways

  • Highway Cruising Doesn't Require Messages: Standard open-highway traffic is predictable from position and speed alone. Asking nobody achieves 0% crashes with zero communication overhead.
  • Intent Messages Prevent Ambiguous Collisions: In sudden cut-in scenarios where driver intentions are hidden until the last second, intent messages cut crashes in half.
  • Safety Guards Prevent Catastrophic Failure: Pairing the neural planner with physical safety guards (speed cap, dynamic stopping clearance, and lane-clearance checks) dropped crashes from 97% to 0%.
  • Oracle Teaching Yields the Best Request Policy: In Experiment 7, training the Request DQN from a privileged simulation oracle slashed cut-in crashes by 40% (124 → 75) while asking 23% fewer questions than DQN6.
