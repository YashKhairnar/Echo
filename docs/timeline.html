PHASE 1 · Highway with normal traffic · Nov 5 – Nov 14
  1. Nov 5
    Set up the world Custom highway, stable car IDs, messages, first world model (MLP)
    What we built
    A custom HighwayEnv with one ego car and ten neighbours, stable vehicle IDs, intent fields, a message format, a random-policy data collector, and the first MLP world model.
    Why
    Everything later needs a world where cars can be identified and asked.
  2. Nov 12
    Exp 1–2: a DQN asks one car per step 5-step planner + request DQN, 2,000 episodes · reward rose, but no baseline
    What changed
    The DQN became a request policy: ask one visible car or nobody, with a cost per question. A 5-step planner used the MLP to drive.
    Result
    Average reward rose from 6.42 to 8.67 after resuming training to 2,000 episodes, and the communication cost fell.
    Lesson
    Without comparing against "ask nobody" on the same seeds, we couldn't tell whether asking helped at all.
    reward 6.42 → 8.672,000 episodes
  3. Nov 12
    Exp 3: ask many cars at once (MLPx + DQNx) One ask / don't-ask choice per visible car · tended to ask nobody
    What changed
    MLPx, a world model that reads messages from several cars, and DQNx, which decides separately for each visible car whether to ask.
    Result
    At a cost of 0.1 per car, the policy drifted towards asking nobody.
    Lesson
    Evaluation needs a no-request baseline to show whether messages are worth their cost.
  4. Nov 13
    Exp 4: time-to-collision messages Messages kept apart from what the car sees · same as asking nobody
    What changed
    Observations only hold position and speed; messages carry intent, target lane and time to collision.
    Result
    On the same seeds, the trained DQNx and "ask nobody" both scored 5.54; asking everyone scored −0.53.
    Lesson
    Communication got cheaper, but there was still no measurable benefit from it.
    DQNx 5.54ask nobody 5.54ask everyone −0.53
  5. Nov 14
    Check the world model against reality Imagined vs real outcomes · planning 3 steps works much better than 5
    What we did
    Compared MLPx's imagined outcomes with real environment rollouts from the same state and action.
    Result
    Planning 5 steps ahead picked the right move only 23% of the time; 3 steps: 67%.
    Lesson
    Imagination errors pile up, so the planner switched to 3 steps.
    5 steps: 23%3 steps: 67%
  6. Nov 15
    Turning point: why did asking never help? Messages said nothing the car couldn't already see, and nothing forced a risky choice
    Diagnosis
    The messages (current acceleration, time to collision) could mostly be worked out from what the car already observes. And on an open highway the car never has to make a decision that depends on them.
    Decision
    Build a scenario where the information is truly hidden and actually changes the best move.
PHASE 2 · Forced merge with hidden yielding / aggressive drivers · Nov 15
  1. Merge scenario + information check Hand-written rules, no learning · asking the rear car: 4.64 → 7.35
    The scenario
    A stalled car blocks the ego's lane, so it must merge into a line of 9 cars. Each is secretly yielding (lets you in) or aggressive (speeds up and closes the gap).
    Hidden?
    A classifier guessing driver type from what the ego sees: 51.4%, exactly chance.
    Useful?
    Without asking: gamble (5.17, 52% crashes) or wait for a safe gap (4.64). Asking the rear car: 7.35 with 0% crashes.
    classifier 51.4% = chanceinformation worth +2.7300 seeds
  2. MLPx v1–v3: crash prediction, 10,000 episodes Drives better overall · but the planner ignores messages
    What changed
    v1: the first merge world model. v2: a "will this step crash?" output. v3: 10,000 episodes of data.
    Result
    Crash prediction became very good (AUC 0.99), but messages barely changed the predictions.
    Lesson
    The data had the signal (merging in front of an aggressive car crashed 76% of the time, a yielding one 31%). The plain MLP couldn't match each car with its own message.
  3. MLPx v4: each car paired with its own message Messages start to matter · asking the rear car worth only +0.66
    What changed
    A per-vehicle network: each car is encoded together with its own message, then all cars are pooled into a scene summary.
    Result
    The planner finally drove better when it asked, but the gain was small.
    Lesson
    A bad merge crashes 1–2 steps later; the planner only saw that through blurry imagined states.
  4. Request DQN, run 1 One car or nobody per step · 6.27, same as asking nobody (6.23)
    What changed
    A new request DQN that scores each visible car with one shared network, like MLPx.
    Result
    It asked rarely and picked the rear car twice as often as random, but drove no better than asking nobody.
    Lesson
    With the planner gaining so little from messages, there was almost nothing for the DQN to learn.
  5. MLPx v5: "will I crash in the next 3 steps?" Catches merges that go wrong later · asking the rear car worth +2.68
    What changed
    A second crash output that predicts a crash within the next 3 steps, from the real observation and the real messages. The planner uses it to judge its first move.
    Result
    Asking the rear car: 8.47 with 0% crashes, against 5.79 for asking nobody. The true value of the information is +2.7.
    Lesson
    The planner now uses messages fully.
    rear rule 8.47ask nobody 5.790% crashes
  6. Request DQN, run 2 Same DQN, better planner · learned never to ask (5.78)
    Result
    It asked only 0.3 times per episode and scored like asking nobody.
    Lesson
    While practising it asked one of ~9 cars at random, so it almost never asked the right car at the right moment. And the payoff came several steps after the question.
  7. Request DQN, run 3: the first one that learned to askNOW Practise on target-lane cars, look 3 steps ahead · 6.77 vs 5.79, crashes 18% → 2%
    What changed
    While practising, it mostly asks cars in the lane it wants to enter, and it learns from 3-step returns so the later payoff reaches the question. 3,000 episodes.
    Result
    Beats asking nobody by +0.98 (about 3 standard errors), cuts crashes from 18% to 2%, and sends 57% of its questions to the rear car (random: 12%).
    Still missing
    It asks twice as often as the rear-car rule (13.5 vs 6.7 per episode) and merges later (7.8 s vs 4.9 s). Its training score was still rising.
    DQN 6.77ask nobody 5.79rear rule 8.47crashes 2%
  8. Next
    Close the gap to the rear-car rule (8.47) Train longer · try different question costs · back to a 4-lane highway
    Train longer
    The training score was still rising at 3,000 episodes.
    Cost sweep
    Train at several question costs and check that the DQN asks less when questions are expensive.
    4 lanes
    The same hidden drivers on a busier highway, where it's less obvious whom to ask.
