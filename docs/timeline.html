PHASE 1 · Highway with normal traffic · Nov 5 – Nov 14
  1. Nov 5
    Set up the world Custom highway, stable car IDs, messages, first world model (MLP)
    What we built
    A custom HighwayEnv with one ego car and ten neighbours, stable vehicle IDs, intent fields, a message format, a random-policy data collector, and the first MLP world model.
    Why
    Everything later needs a world where cars can be identified and asked.
  2. Nov 12
    Exp 1–2: a DQN asks one car per step 5-step planner + request DQN, 2,000 episodes · reward rose, but no baseline
    What changed
    The DQN became a request policy: ask one visible car or nobody, with a cost per question. A 5-step planner used the MLP to drive.
    Result
    Average reward rose from 6.42 to 8.67 after resuming training to 2,000 episodes, and the communication cost fell.
    Lesson
    Without comparing against "ask nobody" on the same seeds, we couldn't tell whether asking helped at all.
    reward 6.42 → 8.672,000 episodes
  3. Nov 12
    Exp 3: ask many cars at once (MLPx + DQNx) One ask / don't-ask choice per visible car · tended to ask nobody
    What changed
    MLPx, a world model that reads messages from several cars, and DQNx, which decides separately for each visible car whether to ask.
    Result
    At a cost of 0.1 per car, the policy drifted towards asking nobody.
    Lesson
    Evaluation needs a no-request baseline to show whether messages are worth their cost.
  4. Nov 13
    Exp 4: time-to-collision messages Messages kept apart from what the car sees · same as asking nobody
    What changed
    Observations only hold position and speed; messages carry intent, target lane and time to collision.
    Result
    On the same seeds, the trained DQNx and "ask nobody" both scored 5.54; asking everyone scored −0.53.
    Lesson
    Communication got cheaper, but there was still no measurable benefit from it.
    DQNx 5.54ask nobody 5.54ask everyone −0.53
  5. Nov 14
    Check the world model against reality Imagined vs real outcomes · planning 3 steps works much better than 5
    What we did
    Compared MLPx's imagined outcomes with real environment rollouts from the same state and action.
    Result
    Planning 5 steps ahead picked the right move only 23% of the time; 3 steps: 67%.
    Lesson
    Imagination errors pile up, so the planner switched to 3 steps.
    5 steps: 23%3 steps: 67%
  6. Nov 15
    Turning point: why did asking never help? Messages said nothing the car couldn't already see, and nothing forced a risky choice
    Diagnosis
    The messages (current acceleration, time to collision) could mostly be worked out from what the car already observes. And on an open highway the car never has to make a decision that depends on them.
    Decision
    Build a scenario where the information is truly hidden and actually changes the best move.
PHASE 2 · Forced merge with hidden yielding / aggressive drivers · Nov 15 – Nov 17
  1. Merge scenario + information check Hand-written rules, no learning · asking the rear car: 4.64 → 7.35
    The scenario
    A stalled car blocks the ego's lane, so it must merge into a line of 9 cars. Each is secretly yielding (lets you in) or aggressive (speeds up and closes the gap).
    Hidden?
    A classifier guessing driver type from what the ego sees: 51.4%, exactly chance.
    Useful?
    Without asking: gamble (5.17, 52% crashes) or wait for a safe gap (4.64). Asking the rear car: 7.35 with 0% crashes.
    classifier 51.4% = chanceinformation worth +2.7300 seeds
  2. MLPx v1–v3: crash prediction, 10,000 episodes Drives better overall · but the planner ignores messages
    What changed
    v1: the first merge world model. v2: a "will this step crash?" output. v3: 10,000 episodes of data.
    Result
    Crash prediction became very good (AUC 0.99), but messages barely changed the predictions.
    Lesson
    The data had the signal (merging in front of an aggressive car crashed 76% of the time, a yielding one 31%). The plain MLP couldn't match each car with its own message.
  3. MLPx v4: each car paired with its own message Messages start to matter · asking the rear car worth only +0.66
    What changed
    A per-vehicle network: each car is encoded together with its own message, then all cars are pooled into a scene summary.
    Result
    The planner finally drove better when it asked, but the gain was small.
    Lesson
    A bad merge crashes 1–2 steps later; the planner only saw that through blurry imagined states.
  4. Request DQN, run 1 One car or nobody per step · 6.27, same as asking nobody (6.23)
    What changed
    A new request DQN that scores each visible car with one shared network, like MLPx.
    Result
    It asked rarely and picked the rear car twice as often as random, but drove no better than asking nobody.
    Lesson
    With the planner gaining so little from messages, there was almost nothing for the DQN to learn.
  5. MLPx v5: "will I crash in the next 3 steps?" Catches merges that go wrong later · asking the rear car worth +2.68
    What changed
    A second crash output that predicts a crash within the next 3 steps, from the real observation and the real messages. The planner uses it to judge its first move.
    Result
    Asking the rear car: 8.47 with 0% crashes, against 5.79 for asking nobody. The true value of the information is +2.7.
    Lesson
    The planner now uses messages fully.
    rear rule 8.47ask nobody 5.790% crashes
  6. Request DQN, run 2 Same DQN, better planner · learned never to ask (5.78)
    Result
    It asked only 0.3 times per episode and scored like asking nobody.
    Lesson
    While practising it asked one of ~9 cars at random, so it almost never asked the right car at the right moment. And the payoff came several steps after the question.
  7. Request DQN, run 3: the first one that learned to ask Practise on target-lane cars, look 3 steps ahead · 6.77 vs 5.79, crashes 18% → 2%
    What changed
    While practising, it mostly asks cars in the lane it wants to enter, and it learns from 3-step returns so the later payoff reaches the question. 3,000 episodes.
    Result
    Beats asking nobody by +0.98 (about 3 standard errors), cuts crashes from 18% to 2%, and sends 57% of its questions to the rear car (random: 12%).
    Still missing
    It asks twice as often as the rear-car rule (13.5 vs 6.7 per episode) and merges later (7.8 s vs 4.9 s).
    DQN 6.77ask nobody 5.79rear rule 8.47crashes 2%
  8. Nov 16
    Longer training, other seeds 6,000 episodes: 6.28 · other seeds: 5.96–6.87 · results depend a lot on luck
    What we tried
    Run 3's setup for 6,000 episodes; the same setup with two other training seeds; and steadier settings (half the learning rate, slower target updates).
    Result
    6,000 episodes did worse (6.28, crashes 13%). The other seeds scored 6.87 and 5.96. The steadier settings scored 6.11.
    Lesson
    The training score swings a lot, and the saved model was picked from a noisy training average.
  9. Nov 16
    Pick the saved DQN on validation roads Same training, better choice of model · 3-run average 6.53 → 7.09
    What changed
    Every 250 episodes the DQN drives 100 fixed validation roads without exploring, and the best-scoring model is kept.
    Result
    Seeds 0 / 1 / 2: 6.92 / 7.45 / 6.90. Every run now beats asking nobody, crashes 1–4%.
    Still missing
    It asks about 18 times per episode, against 6.7 for the rear-car rule.
    average 7.09crashes 1–4%
  10. Nov 17
    Where the DQN asks + cost sweep Wrong car at ~half the key moments · asks 15–20 times or never, whatever the cost
    Where it asks
    At the moments where the rear car's answer changes the move, it asks the rear car only 36–63% of the time, often the front car instead. About half its questions come after the merge.
    Cost sweep
    Trained at costs 0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5 (3 seeds each). Each DQN asks almost every step or never; from cost 0.2 every run stops asking, though asking still pays.
    Lesson
    One useful question is a small signal in a lot of noise, so the DQN never learns when to ask.
  11. Nov 17
    Value-of-information rule: ask only if it changes the movePAST The planner imagines both answers, no training · 8.52, matches the hand-written rule at every cost
    How it works
    For each car in the target lane, the planner imagines both answers ("yielding" and "aggressive"). Asking is worth how much better the ego could drive knowing the answer than with the move it would make without asking. It asks the most valuable car if that beats the cost.
    Result
    At cost 0.05: 8.52 against 8.47 for the hand-written rule. Across costs 0 to 0.5 it matches or beats that rule everywhere (6.78 vs 5.44 at 0.5) and asks less as cost rises: 16.4 → 2.7 questions per episode.
    Fix along the way
    The first version compared against the wrong baseline and merged without asking in 6 of its 8 crashes. Comparing with the move the ego actually makes without asking fixed that.
    Still missing
    It crashes 2–4%; the hand-written rule crashes 0%.
    8.52 at cost 0.05rear rule 8.47DQN 7.09no training
    12. PHASE 3 · Build and test the informed world-model pipeline · Nov 19
  12. Nov 19
    Four-lane intent-aware world model Four lanes, hidden behavior, eight spatial message slots · 26,597 transitions merged
    Data
    Combined informed and random no-message transitions from a four-lane HighwayEnv setup, with aggressive and defensive vehicles and fixed spatial message slots.
    Model
    A compact 116-input MLPx predicts next state and reward only. One-step evaluation is strong; open-loop error grows across three- and five-step imagination.
    Result
    26,597 transitions are available for world-model training.
    26,597 transitions116 inputs3 / 5-step evaluation
  13. Nov 19
    Train the request DQN Ask eight spatial slots or nobody · returns rose, but crashes and loss remain high
    What changed
    The DQN chooses which visible spatial slot to ask. A frozen three-step MLPx planner then chooses the ego driving action.
    Logging
    Each episode records return, mean loss, epsilon, request count, crash, and truncation status.
    Lesson
    Returns improved during training, but crash frequency remains high and the rising loss indicates that the request DQN needs stabilization.
  14. Nov 19
    Render and inspect the learned policy Color-coded vehicles, crash frames, and requested-car highlights · behavior is easy to verify
    Visualization
    The ego is green, aggressive vehicles orange, defensive vehicles blue, and crashed vehicles red. A yellow circle marks the vehicle whose message was requested.
    Tool
    scripts.evaluation.render_request_dqn displays the DQN request and the planner's ego action at every step.
  15. Nov 19
    Experiment 2: collect more data and retrain MLPx 223,375 transitions from 2,000 source episodes · 96.55% three-step no-message action agreement
    Training
    197,553 informed and 25,822 random no-message records. MLPx trained for 50 epochs, with best test loss 0.004263 at epoch 42.
    Evaluation
    Three-step no-message state MAE 0.0213 and reward MAE 0.0422 on 29 valid states. All-message agreement was 68.97%. Five-step tests had 11 valid states per mode; incomplete candidate rollouts were excluded.
  16. Nov 19
    Both DQNs evaluated: safety remains unresolvedNOW 200 episodes per policy, fixed seed range · Experiment 1 outperforms Experiment 2
    Experiment 1
    DQN return 14.955 and crash rate 92%, versus no-request return 13.070 and 99% crashes.
    Experiment 2
    After 1,000 DQN training episodes, evaluation return was 12.326 with 94.5% crashes, versus 11.973 and 93% for no requests. More WM data has not improved complete-system results in this comparison.
    Inspection
    Matching training graphs and an Experiment 2 video are available. The renderer now labels the requested vehicle and shows its exact intent response and the ego action on the frame.
